# MHA Anime Background Art Style
(Research limited to WebSearch result text; no pages fetched. Results are summaries by a search tool, so detail claims are second-hand.)

## 1. How are the backgrounds painted (palette, detail, light, dusk)?
### Takeaway
No primary-source description of MHA's painting technique was found. Secondary fan-press coverage describes a clean, fairly flat daytime look with a repeated saturated blue sky, a duller urban palette, and little time-of-day variation in the mainline series.
### Cited findings
- Fans widely criticise the anime for reusing the same flat blue sky in dramatic daytime scenes, strongest in season 6 (Paranormal Liberation War, Star and Stripe arcs) [widely reported] ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/anime/why-my-hero-academia-anime-s-backgrounds-controversial-explained), [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/mha-blue-sky-discourse-my-hero-academia-blue-sky-debate))
- Know Your Meme dates the "blue sky discourse" to a March 2020 post, growing late 2022 and peaking December 2023 [widely reported] ([KYM](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/mha-blue-sky-discourse-my-hero-academia-blue-sky-debate))
- The manga darkens skies and lowers clouds to convey height/drama; the anime loses this [widely reported, fan opinion] ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/anime/my-hero-academia-vigilantes-fixes-key-controversy-mainline-series))
- The spinoff Vigilantes varies sky by time of day; fans read it as a fix [widely reported] (same Sportskeeda link)
- An AI-style-generator page describes bold primary costume colours against a duller urban palette. This is marketing text, weak [estimated] ([anifusion](https://anifusion.ai/style/my-hero-academia-style-generator/))
### Inferences
- For dusk, the anime gives little to copy directly; the game can do better by shifting sky colour and light temperature (warm low sun, cool shadow) while keeping the clean, low-clutter look and bright costume colours as the focal accent.
- Keep backgrounds muted/less saturated than characters so heroes pop.
### Gaps
- No source found on palette, brushwork, gradient or lighting method; no confirmed dusk-scene analysis. Image-based observation was not possible.

## 2. How does it differ from the cel-shaded characters, and how do they sit together?
### Takeaway
Not directly sourced. Characters are bold, flat-coloured cel art; backgrounds are painted and quieter. Sources only hint at this via the "duller urban palette" remark.
### Cited findings
- Environments described as duller than the costumes [estimated, weak] ([anifusion](https://anifusion.ai/style/my-hero-academia-style-generator/))
### Inferences
- A pixel version should keep a strong value/saturation gap between character sprites and background tiles, with darker outlines on sprites only.
### Gaps
- No staff or critic statement on how character and background integrate.

## 3. Credited background art / art direction and their approach
### Takeaway
Atelier Musa is the main background studio; Shigemi Ikeda (died 13 Oct 2024) was co-art director from season 1. No interview on his MHA approach was found.
### Cited findings
- Atelier Musa credited for background art on the TV series (e.g. eps 3-13 of season 1) and later seasons [widely reported] ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/anime.php?id=17786))
- Shigemi Ikeda: founder/head of Atelier Musa (founded 1986), co-art director on every MHA anime iteration; died 13 Oct 2024 aged 69 [widely reported] ([Anime Corner via blog](https://animepulsereport.blogspot.com/2024/11/art-director-shigemi-ikeda-passes-away.html), [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/anime/news-my-hero-academia-one-punch-man-s-art-director-passes-away))
- Yukiko Maruyama credited as art director on several seasons including season 6, 7 and Final Season [widely reported, per search summary] ([ANN season pages](https://animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/anime.php?id=24773))
- Takeshi Sato credited with background art on the movie Heroes: Rising [widely reported] ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/people.php?id=97466))
- Nami Sato credited for background art at Atelier Musa on several seasons [widely reported]
### Inferences
- Credits are shared per season; verify exact names per episode on ANN before crediting anyone in game materials.
### Gaps
- No staff interview or approach statement found; Japanese-language search (池田繁美) suggested but not done. Bones issued no response to sky criticism in results.

## 4. Official MHA games and reviewer comments on look
### Takeaway
Reviewers praise the cel-shaded character models and faithful anime look; city maps are noted mainly for layout/destructibility, not painted style.
### Cited findings
- One's Justice 2: praised as faithful to manga/anime art style, though the visual approach sometimes hurts gameplay [widely reported] ([Sixth Axis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/05/04/my-hero-ones-justice-2-review/))
- Visuals only slightly improved over the first game [widely reported] ([Ungeek](https://ungeek.ph/2020/03/my-hero-ones-justice-2-review-ps4-going-further-beyond)); Push Square calls it bland yet good-looking ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/my_hero_ones_justice_2))
- Switch 2 version described as clean, anime-like, with sparkling effects [widely reported] ([NoobFeed](https://noobfeed.com/reviews/my-hero-ones-justice-2-review))
- Ultra Rumble: destructible city; U.A. Island stage has downtown medium buildings linked by scaffolding, dense low-rise suburbs with alleys, a wooded zone, rooftops, a beach [sourced, official] ([Bandai Namco](https://en.bandainamcoent.eu/my-hero-academia/news/my-hero-ultra-rumble-new-stage-ua-island)); one review says it reuses One's Justice 2 assets; Steam rating Mixed [widely reported] ([Nichegamer](https://nichegamer.com/reviews/my-hero-ultra-rumble-review))
### Inferences
- Useful for street composition (low-rise alleys, scaffolds, rooftops) more than for colour.

## 5. Translating anime city backgrounds to pixel art
### Takeaway
General pixel craft applies: limited palettes with hue-shifted ramps, sparing dithering, atmospheric perspective. No anime-to-pixel tutorial was found.
### Cited findings
- Distant objects lighter and less saturated; foreground more saturated and darker [sourced, tutorial] ([Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7656))
- Dithering alternates two colours to suggest a third; keep it light for atmosphere, heavy reads as noise [widely reported] ([Divoom](https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/pixel-art-dithering-when-to-use-and-stop), [Megacat](https://megacatstudios.com/ru/blogs/retro-development/dithering-tips))
- Ramps look natural with hue variation [widely reported] ([LPC](https://lpc.opengameart.org/node/20643))
- A foggy night city pixel piece used 6 colours [sourced example] ([PixelJoint](https://pixeljoint.com/pixelart/88670.htm))
- Anime street scenes: learn two-point perspective first [sourced] ([ImagineFX](https://magzter.com/ja/stories/Entertainment/ImagineFX/Compose-An-Anime-Street-Scene))
### Inferences
- Suggested dusk recipe [estimated]: one sky gradient of 4-6 banded steps (warm orange at horizon to desaturated violet/blue), shadows shifted cool, warm rim light on building edges, window lights as small yellow accents, dither only at sky bands.
### Gaps
- No dusk-specific palette guide; Lospec palette browsing recommended.
